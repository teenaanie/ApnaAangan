# Voice

Supplied 25 August 2026 — `assets/guideline-voice.png`. Five traits, in the
guideline's own words:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Warm** | Communicate with kindness and familiarity, making every interaction feel welcoming and approachable. |
| **Helpful** | Provide clear, practical guidance that makes discovering services and businesses effortless. |
| **Trustworthy** | Use honest, transparent language that builds confidence between residents and local businesses. |
| **Community-driven** | Celebrate neighbours helping neighbours, encouraging collaboration over competition. |
| **Simple** | Keep communication concise, easy to understand, and free from unnecessary complexity. |

## What each one means in practice

These readings are ours; the five traits and their definitions are the
guideline's.

**Warm** is about the reader, not about exclamation marks. "Add your name so
they know who is asking" is warm. "Name is required" is not. Warmth almost
always comes from explaining *why*, because explaining assumes the person is
reasonable.

**Helpful** means every dead end offers a next step. An empty state that says
only "Nothing waiting" has told the reader nothing they could not see. Say what
lands there and when.

**Trustworthy** is the trait with teeth, because Aangan asks two strangers to
exchange a phone number. Never overstate what the platform does. Never promise
what the database does not enforce. When a rule is enforced — a rate limit, a
credit limit, moderation — say what the rule is rather than implying something
about the person who hit it.

**Community-driven** is the one an interface forgets first, because it is not
attached to any single control. It lives in the words used for people:
*neighbour* rather than *user*, *the person who makes it* rather than *the
vendor*, and in never framing providers as competing with each other.

**Simple** is not the same as short. A sentence that leaves the reader guessing
has not saved them anything. Cut the words, keep the meaning.

## Rules that follow

**Say what a thing costs before someone commits to it.** The fee appears on the
enquiry, before accept or decline. This is Trustworthy in its most literal form
and it is not negotiable.

**Never write operator copy into a resident's screen.** Notes about seeding
listings, growth or pilot targets belong in a planning document. A resident who
reads them learns the directory is empty and that they are a metric.

**Prefer the specific noun.** "Home bakers, tuition teachers, tailors,
trainers" does more work than "local service providers", and it is warmer,
simpler and more community-driven at the same time.

**No exclamation marks, no emoji in Aangan's own voice.** Providers may use
both in their own listings; that is their voice, not ours.

**Address the reader as you.** "You pay for a customer, never for a listing."

## Assumed, not from the guideline
